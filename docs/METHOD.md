# Method

## Product scope: Brazil nationwide

Field Shift's agronomic and regional-context scope is Brazil nationwide. The system resolves Brazilian coordinates through the versioned municipal mesh and evaluates available municipality/crop evidence; a missing record remains unknown rather than a negative finding. Rio Verde is a reproducible demonstration and artifact example, not the product boundary. Global NASA lookup is retained only as climate-only technical exploration and does not carry Brazilian regional claims outside Brazil.

## Family-farm crops and intercropping

Family-farm crop records and evidence are merged idempotently with the existing catalog. `harvestDaysReference` and harvest-window evidence remain contextual harvest references, never general crop occupancy, local calendar, Kc or water-demand inputs. An intercrop is a system of two or three events in the same plot with overlapping declared periods; succession is a non-overlapping sequence. Documented examples retain their source, constraints and competition warnings. A user-defined system remains qualitative/unknown where evidence is absent. Permanent crops are registrable but excluded from the annual A/B/C generator.

## Versioned regional soil artifacts

Regional soil context is only available from reviewed, preprocessed SoilGrids 2.0 WCS artifacts. An artifact records the exact coordinate, native 250 m support, 0–5 cm interval, source and license, conventional unit conversion, retrieval time, WCS query URLs and the Q0.05–Q0.95 90% prediction interval. The application classifies it as `estimated`, keeps it separate from farmer laboratory data and never treats it as a field measurement.

The current Rio Verde artifact is exact-coordinate only. Elsewhere the soil context remains `unknown`; neither nearest-pixel transfer, municipal averaging nor a browser-side call to the paused SoilGrids REST API is allowed. Brazil-wide rollout is produced offline as reviewed tiles or exact-point artifacts organized by UF, with the same sidecars and integrity checks.

NASA POWER Daily Point uses UTC data from 2011-01-01 to 2025-12-31: `T2M`, `T2M_MAX`, `T2M_MIN`, `PRECTOTCORR`, `RH2M`, `WS2M`, `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN`, `IMERG_PRECTOT` and `GWETROOT`. Sentinels, missing values and implausible values become `null`; never zero. Monthly summaries and rain windows use valid daily values only and retain per-variable coverage/missing-day provenance.

`T2M_MIN` and `T2M_MAX` expose the historical thermal amplitude without creating crop thresholds. `IMERG_PRECTOT`, sourced from GPM IMERG through POWER, adds the median count of days with recorded precipitation and a separate monthly total. `GWETROOT` adds a dimensionless 0–1 root-zone wetness context from the modeled regional grid. Neither rainfall distribution nor root-zone wetness is a field measurement, crop water demand, drought-tolerance score, forecast, or irrigation recommendation. Each enhanced variable degrades independently to `unknown`; the older verified Rio Verde fixture remains valid and does not receive backfilled values.

The Rio Verde fallback has SHA-256, exact coordinates, period, units and UTC metadata. It cannot serve another point. NASA failure outside Rio Verde still returns A/B/C, but climate indicators remain `unknown` with an explicit gap.

The verified Rio Verde fallback was recaptured with all nine parameters, including GPM IMERG precipitation and `GWETROOT`. Its sidecar binds the exact request, units, capture time and SHA-256. No value is copied to Accra, New Delhi or another coordinate.

The Atlas can display NASA SMAP L4 analyzed root-zone soil moisture from EOSDIS GIBS for the fixed, visible date 2025-06-30. It is a 9 km, model-value-added visual layer. A user-drawn polygon now travels with the validated analysis and provides vertex count, approximate area, centroid and a spatial-resolution warning. It does not establish ownership, agricultural land use, field identity or local validation. GIBS colors are never converted into measurements. Numeric historical zonal SMAP L4 SPL4SMGP v8 remains N/D until an authenticated Earthdata preprocessing artifact exists for that polygon.

The catalog is contextualized with Brazilian sources, but their country of origin does not make them nationally applicable. For Brazilian coordinates, a point-in-polygon lookup uses the versioned minimum-quality IBGE municipal mesh, then matches the resulting seven-digit municipality code against the MAPA ZARC 2026/2027 index. The runtime artifacts are generated reproducibly by `npm run build:brazil-context` and retain source URLs, access dates and SHA-256 hashes.

The national ZARC artifact contains only the six current P0 IDs and aggregates source rows by municipality and crop. It retains row count, source culture names, cycle/soil/climate codes, management labels, ordinances, decêndios with non-zero values and the set of raw risk codes. It does not reconstruct correlations lost by aggregation and does not label raw codes as probabilities. Absence from the index means “no matching record in the loaded package”, not unsuitability.

The engine uses a shared planting month (1–12) to summarize NASA climatology, historical monthly precipitation, GPM rainfall distribution and modeled root-zone wetness. These are observations/context, never crop requirements. It returns A/B/C globally: A is confirmed farmer plan or presumed continuation; B deterministically selects a P0 catalogued cover purpose with a different prior family when possible; C, now named “Contexto hídrico”, requires a documented thermal range and a different family when possible. C does not claim water resilience, lower water use or drought tolerance. The water priority highlights root-zone context but still cannot select or swap a crop. All durations stay unknown unless documented.

When a selected crop has a verified stage-specific Kc profile, the comparison adds “Resposta hídrica documentada”. This row places the same NASA exposure (IMERG rain days and modeled root-zone wetness) beside the crop's documented peak Kc and stage. It is traceability, not a calculation: Kc is source/context-specific, studies are not ranked, and the application does not compute `ETc = Kc × ET0` because local/stage-aligned ET0 and a validated calendar are absent. A missing Kc or NASA exposure remains `unknown`.

NASA Insight exposes the trace from one historical NASA observation to the three scenario readings. For each month, the pure engine first compares median daily temperature with each proposed crop's documented viable range. When a value falls outside that range, the interface names the month and hypothesis under attention and shows the NASA value, documented range, rule and sources. It reports a contrast only when available scenario states differ; equal states are explicitly reported as a shared signal. If temperature or thermal evidence cannot support that comparison, monthly precipitation may be shown only as historical observation. It never becomes crop demand, drought tolerance, irrigation need, suitability, ranking, or recommendation. Changing the month inside the insight panel is temporary and cannot change crops, priorities, evidence levels, or the saved draft.

Evidence levels are `climate_exploration`, `regionally_contextualized`, and reserved `locally_validated`. A crop can rise to `regionally_contextualized` only when the loaded ZARC package contains a matching municipality-and-crop record. The MVP never emits `locally_validated`. Priorities only highlight root-zone wetness, cover, or diversity indicators; they do not score or change a selected crop.

The rotation laboratory stores three A/B/C hypotheses with four neutral slots each (Year 1/2 × Season A/B). A slot has no date, duration or implied agricultural calendar. Every configured slot now receives the same pure qualitative rules used by the scenario summary. The first slot compares family with the last historical event; later slots compare only with their immediate configured predecessor. A preceding gap keeps diversity `unknown` rather than skipping backward or inventing continuity. The first configured crop remains a visual scenario summary, not a substitute for the four slot assessments.

After a slot edit, the browser recomputes from the climate and regional context already obtained by the analysis boundary. The feedback is a before/after delta, never an improvement score: it identifies changed states, unchanged evidence and remaining gaps, and can be undone. Saving and opening results causes the server analysis to recompute the same rules authoritatively.

The Evidence Center is generated as a structured manifest, not scraped from UI text. It deduplicates NASA, farmer-input, catalog and municipal sources; links each visible qualitative claim to rule IDs and source IDs; groups missing evidence; and repeats four non-calculation guardrails. Filters may focus the drawer on one scenario, slot or indicator. Counts summarize available layers and gaps only; they are not confidence percentages.

## Climate window by month (ET0, water balance, thermal calendar, what-if)

ET0 is now calculated, and only as a **climate indicator**. `lib/climate/et0.ts` implements the FAO-56 Penman–Monteith daily equation from NASA POWER `T2M_MAX`, `T2M_MIN`, `RH2M`, `WS2M` (2 m), `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN` and surface pressure `PS` (the elevation used by the clear-sky radiation term is inferred from `PS`). Simplifications: soil heat flux G = 0, actual vapour pressure from mean relative humidity (FAO-56 eq. 19), albedo 0.23. The implementation reproduces FAO-56 Example 18 (3.88 mm/day) within 0.06 mm/day in `tests/unit/et0.test.ts`. A day with any missing or implausible input has ET0 `null`; it is never zero.

The monthly water balance takes, per year, total precipitation minus total ET0 for months where every day has both values, and then the median across years (`historicalWaterBalanceByMonth`). It is shown with its coverage. It is context for how dry or wet a month typically is. It is not crop water use (ETc), an irrigation need, a drought tolerance, a yield effect or a forecast; no Kc is multiplied by it.

The 12-month thermal calendar (`lib/engine/season.ts`) compares the monthly NASA POWER median temperature with each hypothesis' documented viable range. Each cell is compatible, attention or N/D (no reading or no documented range) and carries the margin in °C to the nearest range edge (negative means outside). Season insights are rule-based: the month where hypotheses diverge, otherwise the hypothesis with the smallest thermal margin, and the months with a climate water deficit. Every insight states the NASA value, the documented range and the rule; none is a score, ranking or recommendation.

The "what if" sliders (temperature ±3 °C, rainfall ±30%) apply a hypothetical offset to the monthly medians and recompute the calendar, insights and balance. ET0 is not recomputed, the offset is not a forecast, and the decision card keeps using the historical reading.

MODIS NDVI (MOD13Q1 250 m, 16-day, collection v061) is read only from a pre-processed, SHA-256-verified fixture for exact demonstration coordinates (`data/ndvi/`, produced by `npm run fetch:modis-ndvi -- <site>`). The script reads the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC catalog (`modis-13Q1-061`, cloud-optimized copies of the NASA LP DAAC product; Terra items only) and samples the pixel at the point. The ORNL DAAC subset service was tried first and answered HTTP 502/503. Fixtures exist for Rio Verde, Accra and New Delhi (335 composites each, all valid, 2010-12 to 2025-12). The demonstration coordinates are city centres, so absolute NDVI is low (built-up pixels); the seasonal shape is what matters. Elsewhere NDVI is N/D. "Lowest vegetation months" are relative to the point's own series and need a reading for at least nine months; no absolute bare-soil threshold is applied.

Not calculated: ETc, irrigation need, yield, NPK/SOC change, economics, pest diagnosis, forecast, or field diagnosis. Verified Kc is displayed as a source-specific response reference; FAO-56 `ETc = Kc × ET0` remains context only, not an active calculation.

## Farmer-first crops and local soil

The farm draft may include a producer-declared crop profile: crops already operationally available, catalog crops the producer accepts exploring, blocked catalog crops, and named custom crops with a stated purpose (grain, cover, forage, or other). This is operational context, not agronomic evidence. The current/baseline strategy remains visible even if later alternatives are blocked; B and C select only catalog crops that are available or accepted for exploration and never select a declared blocked crop. With no profile, the original global exploratory P0 behavior remains available. A custom crop is retained as farmer context but receives no inferred family, thermal, soil, water, calendar, Kc, yield, or suitability property.

The optional local-soil record distinguishes origin (`farmer_lab`, separately supplied regional estimate, or unknown), pH and method, texture, organic carbon/matter, P, K, sample depth/date, and perceived drainage. All fields are optional. Farmer laboratory values remain the primary local evidence and are not averaged with spatial products. SoilGrids REST is not called because it is suspended; no SoilGrids or MapBiomas Solo regional value is displayed until a compatible, verified, preprocessed artifact with product, resolution, depth, units, uncertainty and provenance is available. Thus regional context currently remains N/D rather than being fabricated.

The comparison derives a separate qualitative soil-health context from the known catalog sequence and local input: number of distinct catalog species, immediate family repetition, catalogued cover purpose, documented nitrogen-fixation potential, initial local organic-carbon/matter observation, bare-soil interval, and erosion-protection context. A bare-soil interval is N/D unless compatible dates, verified duration and management are all present. Cover is not translated into biomass, carbon gain, soil-loss reduction or fertility. Nitrogen remains N/D unless the catalog holds a documented potential; no botanical-family shortcut or kg-N estimate is made. Every state carries missing inputs rather than treating absence as zero.

Every analysis now carries an explicit data-quality contract: `measured` is a producer laboratory record, `observed` is an environmental observation/product, `estimated` is a separately identified spatial/statistical estimate, `derived` is a reproducible calculation, `scenario` is a hypothesis, and `unknown` lacks sufficient evidence. Derived soil-health context stores its method, qualitative unit, inputs and limitations. This classification does not make an estimate interchangeable with a measurement.

## Climate Risk Lab — forecast and dry-spell experiment

The Climate Risk Lab is a separate predictive layer; it does not relabel NASA POWER historical observations as forecasts. `lib/climate/forecast.ts` requests Open-Meteo daily `precipitation_sum`, `et0_fao_evapotranspiration`, maximum and minimum temperature in UTC, with the provider's `best_match` model selection. The response must contain all requested arrays in the documented units (mm and °C). Its provenance exposes the source URL, fetch/issue time, location, model request, units and provider-calculated ET0 origin. A service failure remains a recoverable forecast-unavailable state; historical indicators and A/B/C scenarios remain visible.

`lib/climate/water-pressure.ts` aggregates precipitation, provider-calculated reference ET0 and `P − ET0` only when all days in the requested 7- or 15-day horizon are complete. It also counts days where daily P−ET0 is negative and compares the aggregate with the matching calendar-window fraction of complete NASA monthly historical P−ET0 medians. The balance is a climate indicator, not soil moisture, ETc, crop water use, irrigation need, crop deficit or a prediction of those variables.

`lib/climate/dry-spell.ts` defines a dry-spell event as at least seven consecutive days with precipitation below 1 mm/day. The offline script `npm run train:dry-spell` uses the exact, hash-verified Rio Verde NASA POWER fixture. Each issue date uses only the preceding 30 days (precipitation total and dry days, mean temperature, ET0 total and seasonal sin/cos); the label starts on the issue date. Root-zone wetness is deliberately excluded until a complete, compatible feature series is demonstrated. Samples are chronological 60/20/20 train/validation/test partitions separated by a 30-day embargo. The script writes versioned `data/dry-spell-models.json` with sample counts, Brier score and five calibration bins for both climatology and logistic regression.

Current artifact result (2026-11-14): the 7-day test has 1,059 samples, Brier 0.11025 for logistic regression versus 0.10964 for climatology; the 15-day test has 1,057 samples, Brier 0.09830 versus 0.09381. Neither model improves upon climatology, so both carry `not_validated` and no ML probability is emitted. The UI can show the month-specific climatological reference only at the exact Rio Verde pilot coordinate. No artifact is extrapolated to another coordinate.

Rotation-specific future exposure is N/D because the runtime scenarios do not have verified crop dates, durations or stages. The application states this gap rather than assigning artificial A/B/C risk differences.

## Relief and erosion context (SRTM, rainfall erosivity, relief hotspots)

Relief comes from NASA SRTM GL1 v003 (1 arc-second, about 30 m, voids filled with ASTER GDEM v2; NASA JPL 2013, doi:10.5067/MEASURES/SRTM/SRTMGL1.003), read through the public OpenTopoData `srtm30m` endpoint (100 locations per request, 1 call per second, 1000 calls per day), in one request per analysis. `lib/terrain/terrain.ts` samples a regular lat/lng grid over the boundary's bounding box plus a one-node margin (at least 60 m apart, at most 100 points; a 3×3 grid at 90 m around the point when there is no boundary) and computes slope from finite differences of the neighbouring elevations. A missing elevation makes the dependent cell N/D; it is never treated as flat. SRTM vertical error limits slope at small scales, so the grid is never finer than 60 m and large boundaries are flagged as coarse.

Slope is classified with the Embrapa relief table (Weber et al., Embrapa Uva e Vinho, cap. 5, Tabela 5.3): 0–3% plano, 3–8% suave ondulado, 8–13% moderadamente ondulado, 13–20% ondulado, 20–45% forte ondulado, 45–75% montanhoso (the table defines nothing above 75%, so 45% and up share the last class). A **relief hotspot** is a sampled cell with slope ≥ 8% (the start of "moderadamente ondulado"). It marks steeper relief only.

Rainfall erosivity is the Arnoldus (1980) Modified Fournier Index, MFI = Σp²/P over the twelve monthly totals of a year (NASA POWER `PRECTOTCORR`), taken as the median over complete years, with the classes quoted from Arnoldus/CEC (1992) by Khentouche & Bella (Acta Geographica Lodziensia): below 60 very low, 60–90 low, 90–120 moderate, 120–160 severe, above 160 very severe.

Not calculated: soil loss (RUSLE). It needs soil erodibility (K), cover and management (C, P) and slope length, none of which the app has, so soil loss stays N/D. Hotspots are relief hotspots, not soil-loss hotspots.
